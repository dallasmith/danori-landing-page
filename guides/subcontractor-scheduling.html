How to Schedule Subcontractors When You're Running Jobs Alone

Updated October 2026

Scheduling a sub isn't hard. Keeping it straight across three or four jobs, with no one else watching it, is.

Here's a simple way to do it by hand, and where an app can take some of the load off.

What every sub needs to hear

Send the same five things every time:

  • The address and the start date.
  • The start time, and about how many days.
  • What has to be finished before they arrive.
  • Who's supplying the materials.
  • Who to call if something changes, and how to get on site.

If a sub has to text you back with a question, the message was missing something.

How far ahead to schedule

There's no single rule, and it depends on the trade. Here's a routine most builders can work from:

  1. Pencil in dates early. As soon as a job is won, let your subs know the expected window.
  2. Book it once the date is firm. Two to four weeks out is common. For specialty trades, six to eight weeks is more realistic.
  3. Confirm two weeks out. Check everyone scheduled in the next two weeks and ask whether anything has changed on their end.
  4. Confirm again two days before. One short message with the date, the address, and what's expected.
  5. Check on the day. If they're not there at start time, call. Don't wait for the afternoon.

Trades with a long lead time need earlier confirmations. Quick jobs can use a shorter version.

Make the reply easy

The easier it is to answer, the more often you get an answer. Ask one question and take a one-word reply. Nobody wants to type a paragraph from a ladder.

Look at the next two weeks every week

Once a week, look at the next 7 to 14 days across all your jobs. It's the habit that catches a missing confirmation while there's still time to fix it.

Have a backup for the trades that hold up everything behind them

You don't need a backup for every trade. Pick the ones where a missed day pushes the whole job, and keep a second name for each.

Write down changes, not just dates

When one date moves, everything behind it moves. Tell the affected trades the same day. This is the part that gets dropped most. You're holding it in your head, and when you're busy it's easy to think you'll do it later, and then forget.

Where the load comes from

The texting wears on you too. Scheduling, confirming, reminding, and rescheduling when something changes adds up to a lot of time on your phone, all day, across every job. And on top of that, you're carrying who's confirmed and who isn't. The job follows you home.
