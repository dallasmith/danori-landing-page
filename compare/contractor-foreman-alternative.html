Contractor Foreman Alternative for Solo Builders

Updated October 2026

Contractor Foreman is a good product, and it's affordable. Their own site says it's "created for small and medium sized residential and commercial trade and general contractors," with a starting price of $49 a month.

It has a lot in it: estimates, invoices, change orders, time cards, and connections to QuickBooks and other tools. So the question for a solo builder isn't whether it can work. It's whether you want a big toolbox, or something smaller that tells you what needs you today.

Danori is an iPhone app for builders running jobs on their own. It texts your subs, tracks your profit, and gives your clients a progress page, so no job lives only in your head. Here's how the two compare.

Where Contractor Foreman is the better pick

Pick Contractor Foreman if:

  • You want invoices, purchase orders, and bid management in the same system.
  • You need QuickBooks or other tools connected.
  • You want time cards and a crew schedule.
  • You want the lowest starting price.
  • You want to work from a computer or tablet as well as your phone.

If that's you, Danori isn't the right tool yet, and we'd rather tell you that now.

Where Danori fits

Pick Danori if:

  • You're the one doing the scheduling, the sub calls, the budget, and the client updates.
  • You'd rather have a short list of what needs you today than a toolbox to set up and keep up.
  • You want to see what each job is making before it's finished.
  • You want change orders and estimates built into how you run the job.

A good assistant doesn't hand you 300 emails. They say here are the three decisions you need to make today, and I've handled the rest. That's what Danori is built to do. Your dashboard shows what needs you today. Danori sends the sub texts and the reminders. You make the decisions.

How subs work in Danori

Your subs don't change how they work. Danori texts them what they need to know about the job: who, when, and where. They reply to the text. No download, no login, nothing to learn. You can add a backup sub, and a sub can ask to reschedule by text. Want the by-hand version first? See how to schedule subs when you're running jobs alone.
