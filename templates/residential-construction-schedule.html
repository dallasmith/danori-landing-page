Free Residential Construction Schedule Template

A schedule for remodels and new homes, built by a residential superintendent who's still in the field. The trades are already in order. Type your start date and every date fills in, skipping weekends and holidays.

What's in it

  • Schedule: 26 tasks from demo to final clean, grouped by phase. Days, subs, phone numbers, and a Confirmed column for each one.
  • Dates that keep up: change the days on one task and everything after it moves.
  • Push back: a rain day or a late sub? Type the days and the rest of the job shifts.
  • Same start: trades that work the same days, like the rough-ins, start together.
  • Next 2 Weeks: only what's coming up, with each sub's phone and status, so you know who to confirm.
  • Holidays: a list you can edit. Those days get skipped.

How to use it

  1. Type the job start date at the top of the Schedule tab.
  2. Set the days for each task. The days in the file are placeholders, so use what your subs tell you.
  3. Add your subs and their numbers.
  4. When something slips, type the days under Push back. To pin a task to a date, type it under Start on.
  5. Check Next 2 Weeks every week and confirm whoever is coming up.

To move a task, drag its row to the new spot and the dates update. Sharing with a client? Hide the sub and phone columns first.

The order of trades

The template follows the order most residential jobs run in. Change it to fit yours.

  1. Site and structure: demo, then framing.
  2. Exterior: roofing, windows, exterior doors, and siding, so the house is closed in.
  3. Rough-ins: electrical, plumbing, and HVAC, often on the same days.
  4. Insulation and drywall: rough-in inspection, insulation, insulation inspection, then drywall. Inspections come before anything covers the walls.
  5. Finishes: flooring, cabinets, tile, trim and doors, paint, then the plumbing, electrical, and HVAC trim-outs.
  6. Closeout: final trade inspection, final paint, final inspection, punch list, and clean.

The tasks that hold up the most work are the ones to confirm first: framing, the rough-ins, and anything waiting on an inspection.
