What's in it

  • Budget: 23 line items from demo to final clean, grouped by phase. Type your budget, what each line has cost so far, and what you've paid.
  • Over or under on every line: red when a line runs over, green when it comes in under.
  • Still owed: what you've been billed but haven't paid yet.
  • Contingency left: see how much is left as overruns come in.
  • Projected profit: what the job is on track to make right now, not just what you planned.
  • Change orders: add approved change orders to the contract total.

How to use it

  1. Type your budget for each line. If you priced the job with an estimate, copy your costs from it.
  2. As bills come in, type what each line has cost so far and what you've paid.
  3. When a line is finished, mark it Done.
  4. On the Summary tab, type the contract price, any approved change orders, and your contingency. For a change order, add its cost as a new line on the Budget tab too.
  5. Check the Summary as the job runs.

How the projected profit works

Most budgets only tell you how a job went once it's over. This one tells you where it's headed while there's still time to do something about it.

  • A finished line counts at what it actually cost.
  • A line still in progress counts at its budget, or at what it's already cost if that's higher.

So an overrun shows up in your profit the day you type it in. A line that looks under budget doesn't count as savings until it's done, because the last bill hasn't come in yet.

Contingency you haven't used is kept aside, not counted as profit. If it's still there at the end, it becomes profit then.
